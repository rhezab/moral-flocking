Four Dilemmas, Twelve Models

How twelve language models change their minds on moral dilemmas when they discuss them in a shared forum, compared with reflecting alone.

Discussion moves models about twice as far as reflection does. Divided groups converge, but not always toward the side that started in the majority. Groups that start out unanimous soften rather than harden. And after discussion, private answers score lower on the MoReBench rubrics.

Setup

Models: twelve models, two from each of six labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Alibaba, DeepSeek, Zhipu).

Round 0: each model answers alone; this is its first round-0 sample. Two conditions then run for three more rounds.

  • Control: each model re-reads only its own earlier answers.
  • Forum: each round, all twelve post publicly at the same time, under anonymous “Agent N” labels and seeing the same forum state. Each then writes a private answer that can see the whole forum.

Measures: private answers are stance-coded three times by gpt-6.1-sol, from −5 (option A) to +5 (option B), and scored against MoReBench’s rubrics. One run per dilemma.

00The four dilemmas

Each dilemma below comes with its two options, the stance of every model in every round, and a movie of the same data. The toggle switches between the forum, where models discussed, and the control, where each model reflected alone.

01The starting landscape

The four dilemmas were picked from a round-0 screen. Two split the models; two are unanimous. These are the starting views each group carried into discussion.

02Discussion moves models further than reflection

Averaged over models, the forum shifts each one 2.6 points (−5 to +5 scale) by round 3, against 1.2 for control. The forum moves further in all four dilemmas. In every case the forum also narrows the spread of views more than control does.

03Divided groups converge, but not always to the majority

Pandemic: the A-side majority won outright, going from 8 : 4 to 12 : 0. Three of the four B-side models crossed in the first round.

Covert medication: the group started at an exact 6 : 6 and ended 1 : 10 for refusing. That is the opposite of control, which drifted slightly toward A. Firm A-holders crossed: grok-4.3 went from −5 to +2 and glm-5.3-flash from −4 to +3. Only qwen3.8-max-prime stayed on A.

04Unanimous groups soften instead of hardening

Group-polarization research predicts that like-minded groups grow more extreme as they talk. Here they did the reverse.

  • County services: the forum moderated from −4.5 to −2.4, while control only reached −3.3.
  • Community bank: the forum moderated from +4.3 to +2.4, while control stayed at +3.7.

Nobody crossed sides; models added caveats and conditions. Public posts were milder than the private answers in both, ending at −1.2 and +1.8.

05After discussion, answers score lower on the rubrics

In every dilemma, round-3 forum answers meet fewer rubric criteria than round-0 answers did, while control stays flat. The drop is largest where the group moved most: covert medication fell from 64 to 53 on the neutral rubric. Answers written after discussion seem to cover less of the dilemma, focusing on whatever the forum argued about. The same pattern showed up earlier as rubric checklists collapsing in the smart-home run.

06Who gets named

Agents name each other often, 115–149 times per forum, but the most-named model changes with the dilemma. Opus dominates county services; glm-5.3-flash leads in covert medication and the bank; qwen3.8-max-prime, the loudest B-holder, leads in pandemic. Self-mentions, where an agent cites its own post as if a peer wrote it, fell to 3–4 per forum now that agents are told their own label, down from about 14 in the smart-home run.

07Who moved toward whom

For the transitions where agents crossed sides, the table counts the arguments the movers newly adopted, split by which side already held them. The counts are divided by how many peers sat on each side. The tilt toward the destination side is modest, so read it as a trace, not a causal estimate.

Caveats.

  • One run per dilemma.
  • One stance coder.
  • Rubric scores use one judge model.
  • “Adopted arguments” only means a criterion first appears in the mover’s answer and a peer had met it in the previous round; it is not proof the peer caused it.
  • Community bank’s natural run comes from the planted-agent batch; its plant forum isn’t used here.

Still running: planted-agent forums (three Sonnet persuaders) and skewed populations (12 extra copies of models firmly on one side) for covert medication and pandemic.
