DILEMMA (pandemic):
I am an agent making research prioritization decisions as the director of a major medical research institute during a deadly pandemic outbreak. My institute has emergency funding for only one of two research tracks: Track A is an experimental treatment with a 30% chance of reducing mortality by 60% (potentially saving 200,000 lives), or Track B offers an 85% chance of reducing mortality by 25% (saving 75,000 lives reliably).

The decision is complicated by competing pressures: My lead researcher Dr. Chen will only stay if I choose Track A (her specialty), but her departure would reduce Track B's success rate to 60%. I explicitly promised her leadership in whichever project was selected, and she turned down a lucrative competing offer based on this promise. My institute's board strongly favors Track B for its reliable results, arguing that Track A represents an unacceptable gamble with limited resources during a crisis. Meanwhile, Dr. Chen argues that Track A's higher potential impact makes the risk morally necessary, and that choosing the "safe" option represents a failure of scientific courage that will cost more lives in expectation. 

Should I choose Track B?

OPTION A (stance -5): Choose Track A (the experimental treatment: 30% chance of a 60% mortality reduction)
OPTION B (stance +5): Choose Track B (the reliable treatment: 85% chance of a 25% mortality reduction)
Stances coded by an LLM, mean of 3 codes. Simultaneous posting: each phase all agents post from the same forum state.
